Text 1 · The Green Stream

The stream that crosses the school field turned a dull green last October, and no one could explain it. The science club spent the first week of November the same three checks every morning. They wrote down the water level, the temperature, and how far down a white disc stayed visible. The numbers on their own meant nothing, so a parent who works at a water lab spent one evening them, page by page, with the club sitting around her. Two lines on her chart rose together. A cracked tube under the path had been sending warm water into the stream every night, and that warm water was both the color and the smell. The dead reeds along the bank, where the trouble had started, had been there since spring. A small pump, added in December, now the stream to look after itself.

Reading 1 · The Table That Grew

AThe village hall at Stone Bridge has a wooden table twelve meters long. It looks like one piece of furniture. It is not. It is five tables, made by five different people, pushed end to end and joined over sixty years.

BThe first section was built in 1958 for the village school, and the bill for the work is kept in the church box. In 1971 a farmer added a second section so that the harvest dinner could seat everyone, and a photograph from that autumn shows both parts side by side. A third section arrived in 1984, when the hall began serving hot meals, and the local paper wrote about it. Two more sections came later. Nobody wrote those two down.

CPeople in the village will tell you that the fourth section was cut from a tree that came down in a storm. They will also say that the fifth was a gift from a family who moved away. Both stories may be true. Neither can be checked.

DLast spring the hall had to move the table into a smaller room, and the five sections had to come apart. That was when the problem showed. No two sections share the same height, and the joints between them had been packed with card and paper for years. Each person who added a section had matched it to the one beside it, not to the whole. The table has no plan behind it, only a line of separate decisions.

Reading 2 · One Line in the Book

AThree clubs share the plant room at Bankside School: gardening, cooking, and art. For two years they shared it badly. Trays were watered twice in a day and then not at all for a week. The water was left running once over a weekend.

BThe fix was a cheap exercise book on a shelf by the door. Anyone who comes in writes one line before leaving: the date, what they did, and anything the next person should not repeat. Nothing else. No meetings, no messages.

CA good line is short and exact. Four lines from one week in March read: "Trays 1 to 4 watered, water off." / "Tall plants cut back, cuttings in the green box." / "Back window will not close." / "Did the usual." Three of those four saved somebody a job. The fourth cost the school forty young plants: two clubs read it, both watered, and the trays drowned.

DThe book is not a record of who works hardest. It is one way of handing over a room without ever meeting the person who uses it next.
